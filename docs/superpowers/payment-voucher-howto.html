Payment Voucher Walkthrough

Six screens, in the order you'd actually touch them: set up the masters once, then raise, approve, release, and reconcile every month after. Each section below is one real page — where to find it, who does it, and exactly what to click.

1

Bank Directory

Finance→/finance/bank-directory

The list of banks you can pick from when adding a company bank account. Only needs a visit when your bank isn't already on the list.

Finance Admin
  1. Click Add Bank, top right.
  2. Fill in Bank Name (e.g. "IDFC First Bank"), optionally Bank Code and IFSC Prefix — exactly 4 letters, the first 4 characters of any of that bank's IFSC codes (e.g. IDFB). Typed in lowercase, it's uppercased for you automatically.
    Bank Name*Bank CodeIFSC Prefix
  3. Click Add. The new bank now appears in the dropdown on the Bank Accounts page.
  4. To fix a typo or retire a bank later, click its row → Edit, or Deactivate to hide it from new selections without touching accounts already using it.
→Feeds the Bank dropdown on Company Bank Accounts.
2

Ledger Heads

Finance→/finance/ledger-heads

The chart of accounts — what a voucher or a reconciliation adjustment is actually paying. Eight heads exist already (Vendor Payables, TDS Payable, Bank Charges…); add a new one only when a payment genuinely needs a head that isn't there.

Finance Admin
  1. Click New Ledger Head.
  2. Enter the Account Name (e.g. "GST Payable"), pick an Account Type — Expense, Payable, Receivable, Income, Bank Charge, or Other — and the Tally Ledger Name, the exact name this head has inside Tally.
    Account Name*Account Type*Tally Ledger Name*
  3. Click Create. It's immediately selectable everywhere a ledger head is picked.
  4. Click any row to Edit its name/type/Tally mapping, or Deactivate a head you no longer want offered on new records.
→Feeds the Payable Account dropdown on Payment Vouchers, and the Ledger Head dropdown when posting a reconciliation adjustment.
3

Company Bank Accounts

Finance→/finance/bank-accounts

The company's own paying accounts — the base every voucher and the running bank ledger are built on.

Finance Admin
  1. Click New Bank Account.
  2. Pick the Bank (from the Bank Directory), name the account (e.g. "HDFC Current Account — HQ"), enter the Account Number and IFSC Code, pick the owning Branch, and enter the Tally Ledger Name — kept separate from the account name on purpose, so renaming the account inside HRMS never breaks a Tally export.
    Bank*Account Name*Account NumberIFSC Code*Branch*Tally Ledger Name*Opening Balance
  3. Set the Opening Balance — only meaningful when the account is first created; every period after this, Bank Reconciliation carries the balance forward automatically.
  4. Click Create Account. Click any row afterward for the full drill-down: masked account number, IFSC, Tally ledger, and its audit trail — with Edit and Close Account / Reactivate actions.
→Feeds the Bank Account dropdown on Payment Vouchers, and is what the Bank Ledger and Reconciliation are scoped to, one account at a time.
4

Payment Vouchers

Finance→/finance/payment-vouchers

Raise a payment request. This step only records intent — nothing moves yet.

Finance Head
  1. Click Raise Voucher, top right.
  2. Choose the Purpose: Vendor GRN Payment (clearing what's owed on a received-goods note) or Imprest Float Replenishment (topping up a branch manager's float). A manager already flagged as low on their sanctioned float shows a red Needs Replenishment tag in the picker.
  3. For a GRN payment, pick the specific GRN — its outstanding balance and any TDS already withheld show inline, and the Amount field pre-fills with the suggested net (balance minus TDS). For a top-up, pick the Imprest Manager.
    Purpose*GRN or Imprest Manager*Amount*
  4. Pick the Bank Account it will pay from and the Payable Account (ledger head), then add Remarks and a Reason.
    Bank Account*Payable Account*RemarksReason
  5. Click Raise Voucher. It appears under the Awaiting CEO tab with status badge Awaiting CEO.

Decide the voucher — a yes/no on amount and purpose. Doesn't move any money itself.

CEO
  1. Open the Awaiting CEO tab, click a row to open its drawer.
  2. Review the Approval Progress track at the top and the voucher's details below it.
  3. Click Approve — or type a reason in the text box and click Reject. A rejected voucher stops here permanently; its reason is shown to whoever raised it.
The CEO cannot be the same person who raised the voucher — enforced on the server, not just hidden in the UI.

Actually execute the transfer. The only step that moves real money and writes the Bank Ledger.

Accounts Head
  1. Open the Awaiting Release tab, click the voucher.
  2. After sending the payment through your bank, come back and fill in the Payment Mode (Cheque, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, Cash, Bank Transfer, Adjustment, or Other), the Payment Date, and the Transaction Ref / UTR / Cheque No.
    Payment Mode*Payment Date*Transaction Ref
  3. Click Release Payment. Status flips to Released, and this is the moment that posts the entry into the Bank Ledger — and, for a GRN payment with TDS withheld, a second balancing entry to TDS Payable in the same transaction.
→Appears immediately on the Bank Ledger page for that bank account.
5

Bank Ledger

Finance→/finance/bank-ledger

Read-only running bank book — every released voucher and reconciliation adjustment, in order, with a running balance. Nothing here is ever hand-edited.

Accounts Head / Finance
  1. Pick the Bank Account and a date range at the top.
  2. Scan the Credit/Debit table — each row names the voucher, who raised/approved/released it, and the running balance after it.
  3. Click Export CSV for a spreadsheet, or Tally XML for an import-ready file. The amber banner above the table tells you whether this range is still provisional or already final — see Bank Reconciliation for what decides that.
6

Bank Reconciliation

Finance→/finance/bank-reconciliation

Once a month (or whenever your statement arrives): match HRMS against what the bank actually shows.

Accounts Head
  1. Pick the Bank Account. If there's no period already open, click Start New Period and set the From/To dates — From is pre-filled to the day after your last closed period.
  2. Under Reconcile, choose your statement file (.csv, .xlsx or .xls) and click Read Columns.
  3. Map the columns once: which uploaded column is Date, Description, Reference, and either separate Debit/Credit columns or one signed Amount column. This mapping is remembered for this account's next upload.
  4. Click Upload & Auto-Match. A summary tells you how many lines matched automatically by exact amount within ±15 days — the rest need your eye.
  5. For each line still in Unmatched Statement Lines: click Match to pick the HRMS entry it corresponds to, or Post Adjustment for something the bank shows that HRMS never recorded (a bank charge, interest credited) — pick the Ledger Head and add a Narration. A wrong manual match here won't be accepted; the amounts must agree exactly.
  6. In Resolved Lines, a matched (not adjusted) line can still be Unmatched if you picked wrong — any time before the period is closed.

Close the period — the step that actually locks the books for this range.

Accounts Head
  1. In Reconciliation Summary, type the Statement Closing Balance — the figure printed on the real bank statement.
  2. The page shows how many lines are resolved out of the total. Close Period stays disabled until every line is resolved.
  3. Click Close Period. The server checks the numbers actually balance — HRMS's closing balance, minus anything still outstanding, must equal what you typed. If it doesn't, closing is refused and the exact rupee difference is shown, so you know what to chase down rather than guess.
  4. Once closed, that period moves to the History tab. Click it any time for the full breakdown — balances, who closed it and when, and matched/unmatched/adjusted counts.
  5. Made a mistake after closing? Only the most recently closed period can be reopened — from its drawer, click Reopen This Period and type a reason. A period that an even-later period has already closed over can't be reopened, to protect the running chain of opening balances.
→Once closed, every entry in that range flips the Tally export on the Bank Ledger page from provisional to final.
